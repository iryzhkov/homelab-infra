## Decision: jocasta stays Compose

The plan converts Jocasta only if `jocasta-firewall` is generalised to both workers and to ingress traffic. I do not convert it:

1. Its only clients reach it on homelab's mesh address: the edge route is `url: http://10.42.0.2:1935`, and the firewall accepts 1935 only from the two edge proxies (`ssh homelab 'sudo iptables -S JOCASTA'`: `-A JOCASTA -s 10.42.0.3/32 -j RETURN`, `-A JOCASTA -s 10.42.0.10/32 -j RETURN`, `-A JOCASTA -j DROP`). A Swarm port is published through the routing mesh, and the foundation's ingress filter drops exactly that path (`-A SWARM-INGRESS -i nebula1 -o docker_gwbridge -j DROP`). Making it work needs host-mode publishing, a firewall unit on both workers, and an edge route that follows the pin label, which a single `url:` cannot.
2. Swarm drops part of its hardening: `security_opt: no-new-privileges` is ignored by `docker stack deploy`.
3. Three systemd units are built around the Compose project: `jocasta-container.service` orders start-up after Nebula and the firewall, and `jocasta-backup` and its watch timer read the Compose directory. All three would need rework.
4. It is the agents' working-document store, in use by the lead and executors during this phase, and small and single-tenant: converting it buys little and risks the coordination channel.

The plan's table moves it to compose-pinned (homelab, inventory variable), with its existing `jocasta-backup` hourly unit. Its host_vars entry stays as it is. A homelab drain stops it, as it stops every literal-address consumer.
